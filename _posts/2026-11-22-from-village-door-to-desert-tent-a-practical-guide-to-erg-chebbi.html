Erg Chebbi is the great sweep of dunes beside Merzouga in southeastern Morocco. You can visit for a few hours, sleep at the edge of the sand, or spend a night at a camp among the dunes. Each choice gives you a different day.

Start with the journey you want. Then look at the tent, the bathroom, the meals, and the way your bags will reach camp. A little planning leaves more time for the good part: watching the light move across the sand.

Merzouga, Erg Chebbi, and Your Camp Are Different Places

Merzouga is a village. Erg Chebbi is the neighboring sand sea. A desert camp is an overnight destination, reached from a meeting point rather than simply by arriving at the village sign.

The distinction matters when you book. A package described as starting in Merzouga may expect you to make your own way there. The journey from Marrakech or Fes is a separate matter unless the offer says it is included.

The page on Merzouga village location places the village in Errachidia Province, close to Erg Chebbi. It is a useful map reference, but it does not tell you where a particular camp meets its guests.

Ask for the meeting point and the time you need to be there. Also ask what happens next: a dromedary ride, a vehicle transfer, or another arrangement. Your night in the dunes begins with that handoff.

What the Erg Chebbi Dunes Look Like

Erg Chebbi rises from the surrounding plain in long curves and high ridges. Its tallest dunes stand out sharply against the flatter ground, giving the area the unmistakable shape travelers come to see.

Published measurements differ. A scientific paper from 2024 describes the sand sea as roughly 18 kilometers long and 9 kilometers wide. A local travel guide gives a length of about 22 to 28 kilometers and a width of 5 to 7 kilometers.

Those figures do not need to match for you to plan a visit. They show that this is a substantial area, not a single sand hill beside the road. The local guide puts the tallest dunes at about 150 meters above the plain.

Distance on a map can be misleading in sand. A ridge that looks close may require a slow climb. Give yourself time to walk, stop, and find a comfortable place to watch the sun go down.

Choose a Base: Merzouga, Hassilabied, or Khamlia

Merzouga is the best-known base and a common departure point for dune activities. It works well if you want to arrange a short outing, a camp transfer, or an overnight stay from the village area.

Hassilabied lies about 4 kilometers from Merzouga. It is another base near Erg Chebbi and a useful choice if your plans center on the dunes and the large formation known as Lala Lallia.

Khamlia is about 7 kilometers from Merzouga. Alongside access to the dune area, it offers a chance to encounter the local Gnawa music tradition. A visit there can fit into a broader stay near Erg Chebbi.

The distances between these places are short compared with the long road from Fes or Marrakech. Even so, use the exact meeting point for your booking rather than assuming every activity starts at your accommodation.

Village Room, Dune-Edge Stay, or Desert Camp?

A village stay gives you a fixed base from which to take local outings. You can ride a dromedary at sunset and return afterward, without making the ride part of an overnight camp package.

Lodging at the edge of the dunes brings the sand closer to your room. It offers a different balance from sleeping in a camp: you stay near the landscape while keeping your accommodation at the dune edge.

A desert camp makes the journey into the dunes part of the overnight experience. One listed package includes a private tent, dinner, breakfast, and a ride at sunset. It is designed around one night at camp.

Do not confuse a private tent with private transport for the whole trip. A tent describes where you sleep. It does not necessarily include a car from Marrakech, Fes, or even your chosen village lodging.

Plan the Arrival and Transfer to Camp

 

Your arrival has two parts: reaching the Merzouga area and getting from the meeting point to camp. Write down the location, the agreed time, and the contact details for the person receiving you.

A dromedary ride to camp is not the same as a vehicle transfer. If you prefer a vehicle, choose an offer that provides one. Do not assume that a four-wheel drive carrying your bags will also carry you.

One listed three-day tour from Marrakech allows travelers to request a four-wheel-drive transfer instead of the dromedary ride. That request must be made at least one day ahead for that particular package.

Ask where you leave your main luggage, what goes with you to the tent, and how your bags return. A clear plan for those last few kilometers makes the evening much easier.

Understand the Roads and Distances

Merzouga is roughly 35 to 40 kilometers by road from Rissani and 55 to 60 kilometers from Erfoud. Those towns are much closer than the major cities from which many travelers begin their desert trip.

Fes is about 470 kilometers away. A common route passes through Ifrane and Midelt before following the Ziz Valley south. A local guide estimates seven to nine hours for the drive.

Marrakech is about 560 kilometers from Merzouga. The route crosses the Tizi n’Tichka Pass, and the same guide estimates nine to ten hours of driving. Stops are part of the reason a three-day tour can make sense.

Errachidia airport is closer, at roughly two hours from Merzouga by road. Whatever your route, keep the final camp transfer separate in your plans: arriving in the region is not the same as arriving at your tent.

Choose the Season and Prepare for Temperature Changes

October through April is often recommended for more comfortable conditions around Merzouga. That range includes autumn, winter, and early spring, giving travelers several months in which to plan a dune visit.

The temperature you feel on a walk can differ from what you feel later at camp. Pack layers you can add or remove, especially if your plan includes both a sunset outing and an early start the next morning.

Look at the forecast for the dates of your stay and match your plans to the conditions. A two-hour ride, a walk up a dune, and an evening outside a tent all call for different amounts of time in the open.

Season also affects the rest of your itinerary. Dayet Srij, the nearby rain-fed lake, normally holds water from November to May. Even within that period, rain determines what you find.

Visit Dayet Srij with Flexible Expectations

Dayet Srij is a seasonal lake near Merzouga, also known as Lake Merzouga or Tamezguidat. It is fed by rain and normally holds water from November to May. In exceptionally wet years, water can remain until August.

The lake may also be dry when you arrive. Plan it as a possible addition to your trip, not as the single reason for booking a camp. The dunes remain the main feature of the landscape beside Merzouga.

A biodiversity assessment records about 130 bird species in the area. It also cites a historical count of 400 flamingos in 1996. That number describes one past observation, not a flock waiting for every visitor.

If birds interest you, allow time to look quietly and see what the season has brought. The contrast between a rain-fed lake and the nearby sand can add another side of the region to your visit.

Walk in the Dunes and Learn About Lala Lallia

Lala Lallia is a star dune in Erg Chebbi, also shown as Hassilabied Gran Dune on Google Earth. Its ridges spread from a high center, making it a striking feature of the dune field.

Researchers measured Lala Lallia at about 100 meters high and 700 meters wide. They concluded that it grew to its present height within roughly the past 1,000 years and is moving westward.

Those numbers put a walk into perspective. The dune is not just a backdrop for a camp photograph. It is a large landform shaped over centuries, with slopes that make every step feel different from a walk on a road.

A dune walk is an optional addition to a camp stay. You can spend an evening near your tent, take a short walk, or make a larger climb part of your plans. Leave enough time to return before it becomes hard to see your route.

Hear Gnawa Music in Khamlia

 

Khamlia, about 7 kilometers from Merzouga, is known for its local Gnawa music tradition. A community of musicians performs there, giving visitors a cultural experience alongside the landscape of Erg Chebbi.

Consider setting aside time for Khamlia rather than treating it as a quick stop on the way to a tent. The village adds people and music to an itinerary that might otherwise focus only on dunes and rides.

A stay in Merzouga or Hassilabied can provide a base for exploring the area, while Khamlia offers another place to spend part of the day. The short distances between the villages make it possible to combine them.

Plan the visit around the rest of your schedule, particularly your camp meeting time. A performance and an overnight stay are separate experiences, so check how each fits into the day you have available.

Visit Rissani’s Souk and Try Medfouna

Rissani is about 35 to 40 kilometers from Merzouga and makes a practical stop on a trip through the region. Its souk is busiest on Tuesday, Thursday, and Sunday, especially in the morning.

A market morning gives you a different view of the area from a camp evening. Instead of watching the light on dunes, you can spend time among the goods and activity of a town with deep connections to Saharan trade.

Look for medfouna, a filled flatbread associated with Rissani. Some versions contain spiced meat, onions, and almonds. It is sometimes called “Berber pizza,” though the filled bread has its own local identity.

If you are traveling onward after your camp stay, Rissani can fit naturally into the route. Allow enough time for the souk and a meal rather than squeezing both into the minutes before a long drive.

Follow the Caravan Trail to Sijilmasa

Near Rissani lie the remains of Sijilmasa, a major trans-Saharan trading city founded in the mid-eighth century. Long before travelers came for camp stays, caravans passed through this region on journeys across the Sahara.

Archaeologists have identified remains of a citadel, a mosque, an ore-processing site, and an elite residence. A small gold filigree ring found at the residence is one of the objects that connects the site to its past inhabitants.

In February 1352, the traveler Ibn Battuta left Sijilmasa with a caravan heading south across the Sahara. His departure places the town on a route that linked communities across the desert.

For another view of regional building traditions, UNESCO’s account of Aït Benhaddou earthen architecture explains the significance of that fortified settlement. It is a separate site, encountered on some longer routes rather than beside Merzouga.

Understand Merzouga’s Local History

Many Merzouga residents belong to the Ait Khebbach community. Families who had previously lived as nomads began settling near Erg Chebbi for oasis agriculture from the 1950s.

That history matters when you look beyond the camp. The area is not simply a patch of sand set aside for visitors. It includes villages, livelihoods, and communities with their own ties to the landscape.

International tourism to the dunes grew from the 1980s. Camp stays, dromedary outings, and other visitor activities now sit alongside the region’s longer history of settlement and movement.

Merzouga, Hassilabied, and Khamlia each offer a different way to encounter the area. Spending time in a village as well as at camp helps put the dunes in a wider local setting.

Use a Pre-Booking Checklist

Before paying for a night near Erg Chebbi, put the basic details in one place. The goal is simple: know where you are going, how you will get there, and what waits at the other end.

  • Where is the camp, and where is the meeting point?
  • What time must you arrive, and how will you reach camp?
  • Is the transfer by dromedary, vehicle, or a choice between the two?
  • How will your luggage travel, and where can you leave your car if needed?

Then look inside the package. A private tent with a bathroom is one listed option, but those details should appear in the offer you actually choose. Dinner and breakfast may be included while other meals are not.

  • Are the tent and bathroom private or shared?
  • Which meals and activities are included?
  • Does the trip return to its starting city or end elsewhere?
  • What is the current price for your dates and chosen option?

 

 

Conclusion

A good desert stay begins with a clear choice. Merzouga is the village, Erg Chebbi is the sand sea, and your camp is a separate place with its own meeting point and transfer. Once those pieces fit, the rest becomes easier.

Choose a two-hour dromedary outing if you want a short visit. Choose a one-night camp if you want dinner and sleep among the dunes. Choose a three-day road tour if the journey from Marrakech or Fes is part of the appeal. Each offers something different.

Leave room for the region beyond your tent. Rissani’s market, Sijilmasa’s caravan history, Khamlia’s music, and the seasonal lake at Dayet Srij add depth to the sand. Then come back to the practical details: the route, the bags, the bathroom, and the price. With those settled, you can give your attention to the dunes.
